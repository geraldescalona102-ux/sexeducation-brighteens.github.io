Teenage
Pregnancy
Prevention

a. What is Teenage Pregnancy?

Teenage pregnancy refers to pregnancy among girls aged 13 to 19. It may happen due to various factors, including lack of access to proper sexual education, limited knowledge of contraceptive methods, and inadequate reproductive health information. Early pregnancy can affect a young person's physical, emotional, social, and educational well-being.

b. Teenage Pregnancy Risks:

  • Lacking Prenatal Care

    Prenatal care is important throughout pregnancy, especially during the early stages. Some pregnant teenagers may not receive proper prenatal care due to a lack of family support, financial difficulties, or limited access to healthcare services.

  • Premature Labor

    A normal pregnancy lasts approximately 40 weeks. Babies born before 37 completed weeks of pregnancy are considered premature. Premature babies may experience health problems because their organs have not fully developed.

  • Low Birth Weight of Baby

    Babies born to teenage mothers may have a higher risk of having low birth weight, particularly when they are born prematurely. These babies may need special medical care after birth.

  • Sexually Transmitted Infections

    Pregnant teenagers who are sexually active may be at risk of sexually transmitted infections (STIs), such as chlamydia and HIV. These infections can affect the health of both the mother and the baby.

  • Postpartum Depression

    Teen mothers may experience depression or emotional difficulties after giving birth. This can affect their daily activities and ability to care for their babies. Getting emotional support and professional help is important.

  • Feeling Alone and Isolated

    Pregnant teenagers may feel scared, lonely, or afraid to tell their families about their pregnancy. Without proper support, they may find it more difficult to manage their situation. Having someone they trust can help them cope with these challenges.

c. Contraceptive Methods

Birth Control Pill

Birth control pills are taken daily to prevent pregnancy. Combined pills contain synthetic estrogen and progestin, which mainly prevent the ovaries from releasing eggs. They also thicken cervical mucus, making it harder for sperm to reach an egg. They work best when taken correctly and consistently.

Hormonal Coil (IUS)

The hormonal coil is a small, T-shaped device placed inside the uterus by a healthcare professional. It releases a hormone called progestin, which thickens cervical mucus and makes it harder for sperm to reach and fertilize an egg. It can prevent pregnancy for several years.

Contraceptive Patch

The contraceptive patch is a small patch placed on the skin that releases hormones into the body. It prevents pregnancy mainly by stopping the ovaries from releasing eggs. It must be replaced according to its recommended schedule.

Contraceptive Implant

The contraceptive implant is a small, thin rod placed under the skin of the upper arm by a healthcare professional. It releases hormones that prevent pregnancy and can remain effective for several years.

Contraceptive Injection

The contraceptive injection is a birth control shot given by a healthcare professional. It contains hormones that prevent ovulation and help prevent pregnancy. Depending on the type, it needs to be repeated every few months.

Male Condom

A male condom is a thin latex or synthetic material sheath worn over the penis during sexual intercourse. It prevents sperm from entering the vagina and helps reduce the risk of sexually transmitted infections (STIs).

Female Condom

A female condom, also called an internal condom, is a thin pouch inserted into the vagina before sexual intercourse. It acts as a barrier to prevent sperm from entering the reproductive tract and helps reduce the risk of STIs.

Natural Family Planning

Natural family planning involves tracking a woman's menstrual cycle and identifying the days when she is most likely to become pregnant. Couples avoid sexual intercourse during fertile days to reduce the possibility of pregnancy. Its effectiveness depends on accurate tracking and consistent practice.

Withdrawal Method

This method involves the male partner withdrawing his penis from the vagina before ejaculation. Although it aims to prevent sperm from entering the vagina, it is less reliable than many other contraceptive methods. It also does not protect against STIs.

Vasectomy

Vasectomy is a permanent male contraceptive method that involves a minor surgical procedure in which the tubes that carry sperm are cut or sealed. It does not normally affect sexual performance, erections, or ejaculation. It is not immediately effective, so another contraceptive method must be used until a healthcare professional confirms that it has worked.

Emergency Contraceptives

Emergency contraception is used after unprotected sexual intercourse or contraceptive failure, such as when a condom breaks. Emergency contraceptive pills help prevent pregnancy and work best when taken as soon as possible. They do not terminate an established pregnancy.
