Sexually Transmitted Infections (STI)

a. What is STI?

Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) are infections that can be acquired through sexual contact which can be vaginal, oral, and anal sex. This infection can be passed from person to another person in blood, semen, and vaginal or any other fluids. It can also spread through shared needles or from an infected mother to her baby during childbirth. These infections can affect different parts of human body, some may have been infected already without showing any symptoms.

b. Common Types Of STIs:

  • Chlamydia

    • A bacterial infection that can affect both male and female. It can cause permanent damage to woman's reproductive system which makes it difficult or impossible to get pregnant later on.

  • Genital Herpes

    • A viral infection that causes painful blisters or sores around the genitals.

  • Genital Warts

    • Small bumps or warts that appear around the genital area due to HPV.

  • Gonorrhea

    • It is caused by bacteria Neisseria gonorrhoeae. It is found in uretha (peehole), vaginal/internal genitals, rectum, and throat that may cause unusual discharge and painful urination. It may also lead to serious complications if left untreated such as infertility, difficulty in getting pregnant, and may pass the infection to the baby leading to blindness.

  • Hepatitis B

    • A viral infection that affects the liver and can spread through sexual contact.

  • HIV/AIDS

    • Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) is a virus that attacks the body's immune system, attacking and destroying cells that fight diseases and infections. If untreated, it can lead to AIDS, the last stage of HIV infection.

  • Trichomoniasis

    • An infection caused by a parasite that may cause itching, irritation, and unusual discharge.

c. Ways to Prevent Sexually Transmitted Infections

Avoid sexual activity as it is the only sure way to completely prevent STIs.

Get vaccinated as it can help protect against STIs, such as HIV, AIDS, and more.

Limit sexual partners can also lower your chances of getting an STI.

To be sure, both you and your partner should get tested regularly and be open about your results.

Stay in a loyal relationship. Being with one partner who has also been tested and free from STIs can helps reducing risk of infection.

Never forget to always use condoms correctly every time you have sex with others to protect yourself and your partner from STIs.

d. Testing, Diagnosis and Treatment

STIs are diagnosed through a medical checkup and different tests. A healthcare provider may ask about symptoms and medical history and perform tests such as urine tests, blood tests, cheek swabs, or samples from sores and unusual discharge to identify the infection.

STIs can be treated using medicines such as antibiotics and antiviral drugs, depending on the type of infection. Some can be completely cured, while others, like HIV, need lifelong treatment. Completing the prescribed medicine, avoiding sex during treatment, and informing sexual partners are important to prevent spreading or getting the infection again.
